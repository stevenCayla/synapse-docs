The clinical research terms used in Synapse. In the guides, they are underlined with dots: hover over them to display the definition.

## 2

- **21 CFR Part 11** — US regulation (FDA) on electronic records and electronic signatures.

## A

- **AE** — Adverse event: any unfavorable medical occurrence in a participant, whether or not related to the intervention.
- **AIPD** — French acronym for DPIA, see DPIA.
- **ALCOA+** — Data integrity principles: attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original and accurate, plus complete, consistent, enduring and available.
- **Amendment** — Change to the protocol or study documents after the study has started; a substantial amendment requires a new opinion from the ethics committee (CPP) and, depending on the case, authorization from the ANSM.
- **Analysis population** — Set of participants kept for an analysis (intention to treat, per protocol...), defined in the SAP.
- **Anonymization** — Irreversible removal of any link to the person; anonymous data falls outside the scope of the GDPR, unlike pseudonymized data.
- **ANSM** — French National Agency for the Safety of Medicines and Health Products (Agence nationale de sécurité du médicament et des produits de santé); registers studies (ID-RCB) and authorizes category 1 RIPH.
- **ARC** — Clinical research associate (CRA, monitor; Attaché de recherche clinique in French): checks data quality (monitoring) or helps with data entry on site.
- **Arm** — Treatment group of a study (for example experimental or control) to which participants are assigned, by randomization where applicable.
- **Audit trail** — Tamper-proof log of every creation, modification or deletion: who, when, old and new value, reason.

## B

- **Biostatistician** — Study statistician: writes the statistical analysis plan (SAP), prepares the randomization and analyzes the data.
- **Blinding** — Study mode where the participant (single blind), then also the investigator (double blind), does not know the treatment received; unblinding is when it is revealed.
- **BPC** — Good clinical practice (Bonnes pratiques cliniques in French, GCP in English): the ICH E6 standard for conducting clinical trials.

## C

- **CAPA** — Corrective and preventive action decided after a deviation or a quality issue.
- **CDASH** — CDISC standard for data collection: defines the fields and labels of data entry forms (CRF).
- **CDISC** — Consortium that defines clinical data exchange standards (ODM, SDTM).
- **Close-out visit** — Last monitoring visit of a site, which checks the documents and closes the site's participation.
- **CNIL** — French data protection authority (Commission nationale de l'informatique et des libertés).
- **Coding** — Matching free text (adverse event, medication) to a term of a standard dictionary such as MedDRA or WHODrug.
- **Cohort** — Group of participants followed together over time.
- **Conditional logic** — Rule that shows, hides or makes a field mandatory depending on the answer given in another field (skip logic).
- **Convention unique** — Model contract between an industry sponsor and a French healthcare institution to carry out a study at a site.
- **CPP** — Ethics committee (Comité de protection des personnes in French, similar to an IRB or REC): issues a mandatory opinion before any research involving human participants.
- **CRA** — Clinical research associate (ARC in French): checks data quality (monitoring) or helps with data entry on site.
- **CRC** — Clinical research coordinator: site staff member who assists the investigator in the daily conduct of the study.
- **CRF** — Case report form. eCRF: its electronic version, made up of forms.
- **CRO** — Contract research organization: company to which the sponsor entrusts all or part of the conduct of a study.
- **CSR** — Clinical study report: final report of a study, written from the analysis of the data.
- **CSV** — Two meanings depending on the context: text file of delimiter-separated values (import, export), or computer system validation.
- **CTCAE** — Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events: severity scale for adverse events, from grade 1 (mild) to grade 5 (death).
- **CTIS** — Clinical Trials Information System: European portal for submitting and following clinical trials of medicinal products under the CTR.
- **CTMS** — Clinical trial management system: software for the operational management of a study. It tracks sites, contacts, the schedule and milestones, recruitment, monitoring visits, deviations and payments. It manages how the study is run, whereas the EDC holds the patient data.
- **CTR** — Clinical Trials Regulation, European Regulation 536/2014 on clinical trials of medicinal products.

## D

- **D0** — Day 0 (J0 in French): day of enrollment (or randomization); visits are scheduled relative to D0.
- **Database freeze** — Blocking of data entry by form, visit or patient; queries remain possible and unfreezing requires a reason. Not to be mistaken for the database lock, which concerns the whole database.
- **Database lock** — End of all changes to the database, after cleaning, double signature (data manager then sponsor) and creation of a timestamped snapshot for the exports.
- **Data cleaning** — Handling of discrepancies and queries to obtain a consistent database before the lock.
- **Data dictionary** — Description of every variable of a study: name, label, type, unit, allowed values.
- **Data manager** — Person in charge of data management: designs the database and the checks, handles queries, cleans the data and prepares the database lock.
- **Deviation** — Departure from the protocol observed during the study; it is major when it affects the rights or safety of participants, or the validity of the data.
- **Discrepancy** — Inconsistency detected in an entered data point (value out of range, contradiction between two fields); it may lead to a query.
- **DMP** — Data management plan: describes the database design, the checks, the query workflow, the coding and the lock.
- **DPIA** — Data protection impact assessment (AIPD in French): risk assessment of a health data processing, required by the GDPR when the risk is high.
- **DPO** — Data protection officer: advises the controller on the GDPR, checks its implementation and is the contact point with the data protection authority (CNIL in France).
- **DSMB** — Data safety monitoring board: independent experts who review the safety and progress of a study and may recommend stopping it.

## E

- **eCOA** — Electronic clinical outcome assessment: clinical assessments entered electronically; covers ePRO and assessments completed by the clinician or an observer.
- **eConsent** — Electronic collection of informed consent.
- **eCRF** — Electronic case report form, see CRF.
- **EDC** — Electronic data capture: software that replaces the paper case report form (CRF) with electronic forms (eCRF). It collects patient data, checks its consistency at entry, manages queries, traces every change in an audit trail, collects signatures and allows the database to be frozen then locked.
- **Edit check** — Automatic rule that checks a data point at entry: value range, format, required field, consistency between fields. It triggers a message or a query.
- **EI** — French acronym for AE, see AE.
- **eIDAS** — European regulation on electronic identification and qualified electronic signatures.
- **EIG** — French acronym for SAE, see SAE.
- **Electronic signature** — Signature applied in the software, tied to an identity and timestamped; its value is defined by eIDAS and 21 CFR Part 11.
- **Eligibility criteria** — Conditions a person must meet to take part in the study (inclusion criteria) or must not meet (exclusion criteria); checked at screening.
- **Enrollment** — Official entry of a participant into the study, once the criteria are checked and consent is obtained; in many studies it sets D0.
- **ePRO** — Electronic patient-reported outcome: questionnaire completed directly by the patient on an electronic device.
- **eSource** — Source data entered directly in electronic form, without transcription from a paper document.
- **eTMF** — Electronic trial master file, see TMF.

## F

- **FDA** — Food and Drug Administration: US authority for drugs and medical devices.
- **Field** — Entry element of a form (text, number, date, list, checkbox) that corresponds to a variable.
- **Form** — Data entry page of the eCRF that groups fields around a theme (demographics, lab work) and belongs to a visit.

## G

- **GCP** — Good clinical practice (BPC in French): the ICH E6 standard for conducting clinical trials.
- **GDPR** — General Data Protection Regulation (RGPD in French).
- **GxP** — All the regulatory good practices (clinical, laboratory, manufacturing, distribution...) that apply to health products.

## H

- **HDS** — Certified health data host (Hébergeur de données de santé in French): French certification required to host health data.

## I

- **ICH** — International Council for Harmonisation: body that writes the international guidelines, such as ICH E6 on good clinical practice.
- **Identifying data** — Data that directly identifies a person (name, first name, full date of birth, social security number); excluded or pseudonymized in the exports.
- **ID-RCB** — Registration number of a study with the ANSM, in the format 2026-A00000-00.
- **Inspection** — Official review of a study, its documents and facilities by a regulatory authority. An audit is carried out by the sponsor or its representative.
- **Investigator** — Physician who leads and supervises the study at a site; the PI is the person responsible.
- **IRT** — Interactive response technology (also IWRS): system that manages randomization and, where applicable, treatment supply.
- **ISF** — Investigator site file: the investigator's binder with the essential documents of the study at a site.

## J

- **J0** — Day 0 (D0 in English): day of enrollment (or randomization); visits are scheduled relative to J0.

## K

- **KPI** — Key performance indicator followed to run a study, such as the recruitment rate; a KRI measures risk in the same way.

## L

- **Lost to follow-up** — Participant whose follow-up ends because they can no longer be reached, without withdrawal of consent.

## M

- **MDR** — Medical Device Regulation, European Regulation 2017/745 on medical devices.
- **MedDRA** — Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities: international medical dictionary used to code adverse events.
- **Milestone** — Important step in the study schedule (first participant in, last participant in, database lock).
- **Monitor** — Person who carries out the monitoring of a study (CRA, ARC in French).
- **Monitoring** — Oversight of the conduct of a study to check participant protection, data quality and compliance with the protocol. It is done on site, remotely or centrally.
- **Monitoring plan** — Document that describes how the study is overseen: visit frequency, SDV, critical data and risk indicators.
- **Monitoring report** — Report of a monitoring visit: findings, deviations and actions to take.
- **Monitoring visit** — Visit by a monitor to a site to check the data, the SDV and compliance with the protocol.
- **Multicenter** — Study run at several sites under the same protocol; single-center: one site only.

## O

- **ODM** — Operational Data Model: CDISC format for exchanging and archiving study data and metadata.
- **Owner** — Administration role of a workspace; it is not a platform administrator.

## P

- **Participant** — Person enrolled in a study, also called subject or patient.
- **Pharmacovigilance** — Monitoring of the safety of medicines: collection, assessment and reporting of adverse effects.
- **Phase** — Step of the development of a drug: phase I (first administration), II (preliminary efficacy), III (large-scale comparison), IV (after marketing).
- **PI** — Principal investigator: the physician responsible for the study at a site.
- **Protocol** — Document that describes the objectives, method, selection criteria, visit schedule and analysis of a study.
- **Pseudonymization** — Replacement of identifying data by a code; re-identification remains possible with a key kept separately, so the data is still personal data under the GDPR.
- **PV** — Minutes (procès-verbal in French): signed record of an acceptance test or a meeting; also pharmacovigilance.

## Q

- **Query** — Question sent to the site about a doubtful or missing data point: opened, answered, then closed.

## R

- **Randomization** — Random assignment of participants to the arms of a study to obtain comparable groups. It can be stratified on certain factors and done in blocks.
- **RBQM** — Risk-based quality management: monitoring targeted according to the risk of sites and data.
- **Recruitment** — Phase where participants are identified and enrolled; the recruitment rate compares actual and planned enrollments.
- **Registry** — Organized collection of health data on a population, without an intervention protocol.
- **Requête** — French term for a query: question sent to the site about a doubtful or missing data point, opened, answered, then closed.
- **Retrospective** — Study on data that has already been collected; a prospective study collects it as follow-up goes.
- **RGPD** — French acronym for GDPR, the General Data Protection Regulation.
- **RIPH** — Research involving the human person (Recherche impliquant la personne humaine, Jardé law): category 1 (interventional), 2 (minimal risk) or 3 (observational).
- **RNIPH** — Research not involving the human person (Recherche n'impliquant pas la personne humaine), for example on data that has already been collected.
- **Role** — Set of rights given to a user (for example investigator, CRA, data manager, sponsor) that determines what they see and do.

## S

- **SAE** — Serious adverse event: an adverse event that results in death, is life-threatening, requires or prolongs hospitalization, causes significant or lasting disability, or a congenital anomaly.
- **SAP** — Statistical analysis plan, written before the analysis and before the database lock.
- **Screening** — Step before enrollment: consent, checking of criteria and baseline examinations. A person who is not kept is a screen failure.
- **SDTM** — Study Data Tabulation Model: CDISC format for tabulated data for regulatory submission.
- **SDV** — Source data verification: comparison of the entered data with the patient's source record.
- **Site** — Institution or department where the investigator and their team enroll and follow participants.
- **SIV** — Site initiation visit: trains the site team and checks its resources before the first participant.
- **SOP** — Standard operating procedure: describes a recurring task.
- **Source document** — Document where a data point is first recorded (medical record, lab result, imaging); the reference for SDV.
- **Sponsor** — Person or organization that takes the initiative of a study, is responsible for it and ensures its funding or organization (promoteur in French).
- **Stratification** — Distribution of participants according to one or more factors (age, stage) to balance the arms during randomization.
- **Study member** — User attached to a study, with their own rights; a site member is attached to a site.
- **SUSAR** — Suspected unexpected serious adverse reaction: serious, unexpected adverse reaction suspected to be related to the treatment, to be reported urgently to the authorities.

## T

- **TC** — Test case: test case of an acceptance test, with its identifier (for example TC-08), its steps and the expected result.
- **TEC** — French job title for a clinical study technician (technicien d'études cliniques): supports the site team with data entry and the administrative follow-up of the study.
- **TLF** — Tables, listings and figures produced by the statistical analysis.
- **TMF** — Trial master file: the essential documents that show the study is run according to the rules.
- **TOTP** — Time-based one-time password: single-use code renewed every 30 seconds by an authenticator app, used as a second login factor.

## U

- **UAT** — User acceptance testing: validates that the software meets the need before it goes live.
- **Unblinding** — Revealing the treatment assigned to a participant, in an emergency or at the end of the study.

## V

- **Validation** — Documented evidence that a computerized system does what is expected in a reproducible way (IQ, OQ, PQ).
- **Variable** — Data point measured or collected in a study, described in the data dictionary.
- **Visit** — Planned meeting between a participant and the study team, at a date set relative to D0; an unscheduled visit is not planned in the protocol.
- **Visit window** — Interval around the theoretical date of a visit (for example D28 ± 3 days) in which it remains compliant with the protocol.

## W

- **WHODrug** — International drug dictionary used to code the medications entered.
- **Withdrawal of consent** — Decision of a participant to leave the study; the data already collected is handled according to the protocol and the regulation.
- **Workspace** — Workspace of an organization (tenant): fully isolated environment whose users, studies and audit logs are never shared with another workspace.
